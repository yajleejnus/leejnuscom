📝 Grammar Basics

Hmong grammar is surprisingly accessible for English speakers — no conjugations, no genders!

Word Order

Hmong follows Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) order, just like English:

  • Kuv (I) xam (eat) zaj (food)
  • "I eat food"
  • Koj (you) xam (eat) zaj tsuab (meat)
  • "You eat meat"

Tones

Hmong has 7 distinct tones that change word meaning:

  • Normal — no mark (e.g., ib)
  • Sharp — apostrophe (e.g., ib')
  • Low falling — tilde (e.g., ib~)
  • High rising — grave accent (e.g., ib`)
  • Broken — final consonant (e.g., ibx)
  • Heavy — final consonant (e.g., ibq)
  • Glottal — final glottal stop (e.g., ibh)

Question Words

  • Cov — What?
  • Cov li — How?
  • Cov nram — Where?
  • Cov npe — Who?
  • Cov yam — Why?
  • Cov thaum — When?

Negation

Simply add "tsis" before the verb:

  • Xam = eat → Tsis xam = don't eat
  • Nyob = live/be → Tsis nyob = don't live
  • Yog = is → Tsis yog = is not

💬 Everyday Conversations

Practice common dialogues you'll encounter in daily Hmong life.

Meeting Someone

A: Nyob zoo! [nyob zow]

B: Nyob zoo! [nyob zow]

A: Koj lub npe li? [kow loob nee pee lee?]

B: Kuv lub npe yog [koo loob nee yong]... Koj lub npe li?

A: Kuv lub npe yog [koo loob nee yong]... Ua tsaug! [ow tsowg]

At the Market

A: Zaj no muaj dab tsi? [zahj no mooj dab tee?]

B: Zaj no muaj zaj tsuaj thiab zaj tsuab. [zahj no mooj...]

A: Zaj no ntau npaum li cas? [zahj no naw pawm lee kas?]

B: Zaj no ntau ib plig peb tuag. [zahj no naw ib plig peb...]

Asking About Family

A: Koj muaj tus neeg muaj ntau npaum li cas? [kow mooj...]

B: Kuv muaj peb tus neeg. [koo mooj peb tus neeg]

A: Koj tus vaj nyob li? [kow tus vahj nyob lee?]

B: Koj tus vaj nyob hauv teb chaws. [kow tus vahj nyob howv...]

🎯 Practice Tip: Try speaking these aloud with a partner. Hmong tones are best learned by ear — listen to native speakers and mimic their intonation patterns. The more you practice speaking, the more natural the tones will become!
